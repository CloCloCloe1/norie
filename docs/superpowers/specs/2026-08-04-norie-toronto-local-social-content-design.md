# Norie Toronto Local Sales and Social Content Design

## Goal

Build awareness and sales among Toronto women aged 18–30, across ethnic backgrounds, while making it clear that Norie products can be bought either ready-made or personalized with a name.

## Positioning

**A Toronto accessories and gifting brand: shop the piece as-is, or make it personal with your name.**

Content should lead with an attractive, useful product and use customization as the memorable differentiator. The working content mix is 60% product and lifestyle, 40% personalization and gifting.

## Local Sales Funnel

Every profile and selling post should answer three questions quickly:

1. What is it? Customizable combs and claw clips.
2. Where is it? Toronto, with local pickup or delivery information stated explicitly.
3. How do I buy? A single website link or a clear DM call to action.

Use English as the primary language for Instagram and TikTok. Chinese can appear as a second caption or occasional subtitle, especially on Xiaohongshu, without making the account look limited to Chinese-speaking buyers.

Priority acquisition routes:

1. Toronto-tagged short-form video and creator collaborations.
2. Small local pop-ups that provide both sales and reusable customer footage.
3. Partnerships with complementary businesses such as nail artists, hairstylists, photographers, florists, gift-box makers, university clubs, and micro-creators.
4. Seasonal gifting moments: birthdays, graduations, bridesmaids, back-to-school, Christmas, and friendship gifts.

Do not judge pop-ups only by same-day revenue. Track sales, qualified followers, email/DM leads, custom-order requests, and the amount of useful customer content captured.

## Three Repeatable Compositions

### 1. Clean Flat Lay

- Camera: phone directly above a table.
- Background: white, cream, pale pink, or light stone.
- Layout: product placed slightly off-centre; packaging or ribbon in one corner; one small prop only.
- Capture: one full product shot, one name detail, one hand entering frame.
- Best for: carousels, product availability, gift guides, and ready-to-buy items.

### 2. Hands at Work

- Camera: phone fixed at a 45-degree angle or directly overhead.
- Action: select letters, apply the name, check sparkle, close the box, tie ribbon.
- Framing: only hands, product, and work surface; avoid a busy room in the background.
- Best for: satisfying 7–12 second videos and customization reveals.

### 3. Mirror or Friend Shot

- Camera: chest-up vertical frame near a bright window.
- Action: clip the hair, remove a comb from a bag, or hand the finished gift to a friend.
- Capture: one wide view, one hair close-up, one reaction.
- Best for: scale, real-life styling, gifting emotion, and social proof.

## Six Initial Posts

### Post 1 — Buy It Plain or Make It Yours

- Hook on screen: `Two ways to shop Norie in Toronto`
- Shots: plain product; the same style with a name; both together.
- Caption: `Wear it as-is or add your name in sparkle. Which one are you? Toronto pickup/delivery available.`
- CTA: comment `plain` or `personalized`.

### Post 2 — A Toronto Order in 10 Seconds

- Hook: `Packing a custom Toronto order with me`
- Shots: product; name application; sparkle close-up; box closing.
- Caption: `Made in Toronto for [first name]. Custom and ready-to-shop pieces available.`
- CTA: `DM “TORONTO” for ordering details` until the website flow is frictionless.

### Post 3 — What Fits in Her Bag

- Hook: `The prettiest thing in my everyday bag`
- Shots: bag flat lay; Norie comb removed; quick hair touch-up; name close-up.
- Caption: focus on usefulness first, personalization second.
- CTA: save for birthday-gift inspiration.

### Post 4 — Gift Reaction

- Hook: `She did not expect to see her name`
- Shots: closed box; friend opens it; reaction; final product close-up.
- Caption: `A small personalized gift that does not feel generic.`
- CTA: tag the friend who would love one.

### Post 5 — Toronto Street Test

- Hook: `Which one would Toronto girls choose?`
- Shots: hold a pink and white option toward the camera; two or three quick choices; result on screen.
- Caption: include the neighbourhood or recognizable Toronto location naturally.
- CTA: vote pink or white.

### Post 6 — One Product, Three Occasions

- Hook: `One personalized gift, three occasions`
- Shots: birthday card; graduation ribbon; bridesmaid-style gift box, all using the same product framing.
- Caption: list the three occasions and ordering lead time.
- CTA: save this gift idea.

## One-Shoot Workflow

Film once in vertical 9:16 and keep important text away from the top and bottom interface areas. From one 45-minute session, capture:

- three clean flat lays;
- one full personalization process;
- one packaging sequence;
- one mirror styling sequence;
- five close-ups of sparkle, lettering, teeth, hinge, and packaging.

Turn this material into two short videos, one Instagram carousel, several Stories, one Xiaohongshu post, and one Douyin/TikTok variation. Change the first line, caption language, music, and CTA by platform instead of uploading an identical watermarked export.

## Platform Roles

- **Instagram:** polished brand home, carousels, customer proof, local availability, collaborations, and Stories that answer purchase questions.
- **TikTok:** fast hooks, personalization processes, real reactions, comparisons, and founder-led Toronto content.
- **Xiaohongshu:** Chinese-language Toronto gift searches, local pickup information, price/ordering clarity, and lifestyle recommendations.
- **Douyin:** reuse the strongest process and transformation concepts; keep effort lower until it produces relevant demand.

## Toronto Signals

Use real local signals rather than only adding `#Toronto`: say Toronto in the spoken hook and on-screen text, add a location tag, show recognizable but uncomplicated neighbourhood context, state pickup/delivery terms, and collaborate with accounts whose audience is already in the GTA.

For in-person validation, start with one curated market rather than committing to a long run. Toronto Made Market accepts applications from Toronto/GTA brands and evaluates product fit, originality, presentation, and capacity. STACKT is another relevant environment for rotating local brands and pop-ups. Confirm fees, insurance, dates, and category exclusivity before paying.

## Measurement

Run the first six posts over three weeks and record:

- Toronto/GTA share of reach where platform analytics provide it;
- profile visits and website clicks;
- DMs that mention Toronto or pickup;
- saves and shares;
- ready-made versus personalized inquiries;
- completed local orders.

The first decision after three weeks is not which post received the most views. It is which format produced the most Toronto-based buying signals, then repeat that format with a new product, name, or gifting occasion.

## Guardrails

- Do not make every post look like an advertisement.
- Do not show too many props or more than two product choices in one short video.
- Do not hide price, ordering method, turnaround time, or location once a viewer shows buying intent.
- Do not describe every item as custom; visibly label ready-made products as available now.
- Obtain permission before reposting customer faces or reactions.
